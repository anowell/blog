## Terrestrial Spacetime

<!-- Boringly solar time -->

- *Placeholder:* solar noon is always .500, for everybody
- *Placeholder:* sunrise and sunset are the same for everybody at the same latitude
- *Placeholder:* makes it clear that time is always relative to a location
- *Placeholder:* no more DST: the clock already tracks the sun; "open at .300 in summer" becomes an explicit local choice, not a law imposed on everyone
- *Placeholder:* offsets between places never change seasonally: LA is always .123 behind NY (no weeks where US and EU DST switches disagree)
- *Placeholder:* time zone fairness: the western edge of a zone gets later sunsets and less sleep; solar time has no edges. [Giuntella & Mazzonna 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jhealeco.2019.03.007): ~19 min less sleep per extra hour of evening light, with health and income effects, at US time zone borders
- *Placeholder:* sleep researchers already want clocks closer to the sun: [AASM](https://aasm.org/advocacy/position-statements/permanent-standard-time-is-the-optimal-choice-for-health-and-safety/) (also SRBR, NSF, SRS, AMA) back permanent standard time; solar time is that taken to its logical end
- *Placeholder:* times and durations are the same kind of number: .600 to .625 is 2.5 cd, by subtraction; no AM/PM, no 12 vs 24
